# Writing Rules for Project Descriptions
 
## Structure
 
1. **First sentence is a one-line summary.** It should tell the reader what the project is before anything else. No context, no backstory — just what it does.
2. **One paragraph, 3–5 sentences.** Pick the most interesting details. You don't need to list everything.
3. **End with the most interesting or surprising thing.** The last sentence is what sticks. A good punchline, an unexpected lesson, or a consequence of the project works well here.
## Language
 
4. **Write in first person.** Use "I built", "I replaced", "I designed" — not "the project does" or "it was written in".
5. **Use simple sentences.** One idea per sentence. Avoid relative clauses and parenthetical asides mid-sentence.
6. **No jargon without a hint.** Technical terms are fine, but give the reader a fighting chance. A brief plain-English hint goes a long way.
## Content
 
7. **Be accurate about chronology.** Don't mention a later project as context for an earlier one. If the connection is worth noting, phrase it as a consequence: "This project later became the foundation for X."
8. **Don't explain what you planned but didn't finish.** Either own the incomplete state matter-of-factly, or leave it out. Don't apologise for it.
9. **No throat-clearing.** Cut openers like "This is my first project using…" or "The aim of this project was to…". Lead with what it is, not meta-commentary about it.
10. **Keep hardware details precise.** If there are 32 chips daisy-chained, say 32 chips daisy-chained — not "chips arranged in rows".
